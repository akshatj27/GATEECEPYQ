			[latex]\because[/latex] Given integral is independent of contour ' [latex]C[/latex] ' [latex]\int_{C}\left(F_{1} d x+F_{2} d y+F_{3} d z\right)=\int_{C} \bar{F} \cdot \overline{d r}[/latex] is independent of ' [latex]C[/latex] '

<br> [latex]
\begin{array}{ll}
\therefore & \bar{F}=\overline{\nabla \phi} \\
\Rightarrow & F_{1}=\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x}, F_{2}=\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y}, F_{3}=\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z}
\end{array}

[latex]\therefore[/latex] Option is true.
<br>
[latex]\because \bar{F}[/latex] is conservative
<br>
Then,
<br> [latex]
\nabla \times \bar{F}=0
[/latex]<br>
[latex]\Rightarrow \quad\left|\begin{array}{ccc}i & j & K \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ F_{1} & F_{2} & F_{3}\end{array}\right|=\overrightarrow{0}[/latex]<br>
[latex]=i\left(\frac{\partial F_{3}}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial F_{2}}{\partial z}\right)-j\left(\frac{\partial F_{3}}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial z}\right)+K\left(\frac{\partial F_{2}}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial y}\right)=0 \hat{i}+0 \hat{j}+0 \hat{k}[/latex]
<br>
Equate on both sides,
<br>
We get,
<br> [latex]
\frac{\partial F_{3}}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial F_{2}}{\partial z}, \quad \frac{\partial F_{3}}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial z}, \quad \frac{\partial F_{2}}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial y}
[/latex]<br>
[latex]\therefore[/latex] Option is true.
<br>
If [latex]\bar{F}[/latex] is irrotational then,
<br> [latex]
\oint_{C} F_{1} d x+F_{2} d y+F_{3} d z=0
[/latex]<br>
[latex]\therefore[/latex] Option is also true.
But,
<br> [latex]
\bar{\nabla} \cdot \bar{F}=\bar{\nabla} \cdot(\bar{\nabla} \phi) \neq 0
[/latex]<br>
[latex]\therefore[/latex] Option is false.
